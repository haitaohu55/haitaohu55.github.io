    1. Exact Diagonalization

    We begin with the time-independent Schrödinger equation \begin{align} H \ket{\psi} = E \ket{\psi} , \label{eq1} \end{align} The wave function $\ket{\psi}$ can be expanded in an orthonormal basis: \begin{align} \ket{\psi} = \sum_n C_n \ket{n}, \braket{n | m} = \delta_{nm} . \label{eq2} \end{align} We therefore obtain \begin{align} H & \ket{\psi} \nonumber \\ & \downarrow \nonumber \\ H \sum_n & C_n \ket{n} \nonumber \\ & \downarrow \nonumber \\ \sum_n C_n E \ket{n} & = \sum_n C_n H \ket{n} \nonumber \\ & \downarrow \nonumber \\ \sum_{n} C_n E \braket{m | n} & = \sum_{n} C_n \bra{m} H \ket{n} \nonumber \\ & \downarrow \nonumber \\ E C_m & = \sum_{n} H_{mn} C_n \nonumber \\ \label{eq3} \end{align} More explicitly, this gives the matrix equation \begin{equation} \begin{pmatrix} H_{1,1} & H_{1,2} & \cdots & H_{1,N} \\ H_{2,1} & H_{2,2} & \cdots & H_{2,N}\\ \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots \\ H_{N,1} & H_{N,2} & \cdots & H_{N,N} \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} C_1 \\ C_2 \\ \vdots \\ C_N \end{pmatrix} = E \begin{pmatrix} C_1 \\ C_2 \\ \vdots \\ C_N \end{pmatrix} \label{eq4} \end{equation} Diagonalizing the Hamiltonian matrix $H$ yields its eigenenergies. Here, $H_{m,n}$ is no longer an operator but a matrix element obtained after projection onto the chosen basis.

    2. Number Representation

    Consider the Hamiltonian of a one-dimensional harmonic oscillator: \begin{align} H = \frac{p^2}{2m} + \frac{1}{2}m \omega^2 x^2 , \label{eq5} \end{align} We introduce the creation and annihilation operators (essentially to rewrite the Hamiltonian in the form $a^{\dagger}a + \frac{1}{2}$): \begin{align} a & = \sqrt{\frac{m \omega}{2 \hbar}} x + \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 m \hbar \omega}} ip \nonumber \\ a^{\dagger} & = \sqrt{\frac{m \omega}{2 \hbar}} x - \frac{1}{\sqrt{2 m \hbar \omega}} ip \nonumber \\ \label{eq6} \end{align} The Hamiltonian can then be rearranged as \begin{align} H =\hbar \omega (a^{\dagger}a + \frac{1}{2}) . \label{eq7} \end{align} where $a^{\dagger}a$ is the number operator $\hat{n}$.

    3. One-Dimensional Fermion Chain

    We now consider a one-dimensional fermion chain whose second-quantized Hamiltonian is \begin{align} \hat{H} = \sum_{i=1}^L \left[\mu \hat{n}_i + U \hat{n}_i \hat{n}_{i+1} + t (\hat{c}_i^{\dagger} \hat{c}_{i+1} + {\rm H.c.})\right] \end{align} Let the chain length be 5. We first neglect the interaction term $U$ (equivalently, one may consider the sector with exactly one particle) and impose periodic boundary conditions. The quadratic part of $\hat{H} =$ can be written as \begin{align} \hat H = \sum_{i,j} \hat c_i^\dagger h_{ij} \hat c_j \end{align} where the matrix $h$ is \begin{equation} \begin{array}{c|cccccc} & \hat{c}_1 & \hat{c}_2 & \hat{c}_3 & \hat{c}_4 & \hat{c}_5 \\ \hline \hat{c}_1^{\dagger} & \mu & t & 0 & 0 & t \\ \hat{c}_2^{\dagger} & t & \mu & t & 0 & 0 \\ \hat{c}_3^{\dagger} & 0 & t & \mu & t & 0 \\ \hat{c}_4^{\dagger} & 0 & 0 & t & \mu & t \\ \hat{c}_5^{\dagger} & t & 0 & 0 & t & \mu \\ \end{array} \label{eq9} \end{equation} Note that $\hat{H}$ is still an operator at this stage; the expression above gives its coefficient matrix in the $\{\hat c_i\}$ basis. We next choose the Hilbert space spanned by $$\ket{1,0,0,0,0}, \cdots, \ket{0,0,0,0,1}$$ This space has dimension 5. After projection, the matrix $H$ is identical to the one above; once this correspondence is familiar, we may write it directly as \begin{equation} \begin{array}{c|cccccc} & \ket{1,,,,} & \ket{,1,,,} & \ket{,,1,,} & \ket{,,,1,} & \ket{,,,,1} \\ \hline \bra{1,,,,} & \mu & t & 0 & 0 & t \\ \bra{,1,,,} & t & \mu & t & 0 & 0 \\ \bra{,,1,,} & 0 & t & \mu & t & 0 \\ \bra{,,,1,} & 0 & 0 & t & \mu & t \\ \bra{,,,,1} & t & 0 & 0 & t & \mu \\ \end{array} \label{eq10} \end{equation} Including interactions makes the problem considerably more complicated. The full Fock space has dimension $2^5$. Because the Hamiltonian conserves the total particle number, it decomposes into mutually uncoupled particle-number blocks, so we can work directly within a fixed-particle-number subspace. For example, in the two-particle sector the dimension is $\binom{5}{2}=10$ (the interaction term vanishes in the one-particle sector). The operators then act according to \begin{align} c_i \ket{n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_i, \ldots, n_L} = (-1)^{\sum_{j< i} n_j} \sqrt{n_i} \ket{n_1, n_2, \ldots, n_i - 1, \ldots, n_L} \end{align} Using the following ordering of basis states, we obtain

    \begin{equation} \small \begin{array}{c|cccccccccc} & \ket{1,1,,,} & \ket{1,,1,,} & \ket{1,,,1,} & \ket{1,,,,1} & \ket{,1,1,,} & \ket{,1,,1,} & \ket{,1,,,1} & \ket{,,1,1,} & \ket{,,1,,1} & \ket{,,,1,1} \\ \hline \bra{1,1,,,} & 2\mu+U & t & & & & & -t & & & \\ \bra{1,,1,,} & t & 2\mu & t & & t & & & & -t & \\ \bra{1,,,1,} & & t & 2\mu & t & & t & & & & -t \\ \bra{1,,,,1} & & & t & 2\mu+U & & & t & & & \\ \bra{,1,1,,} & & t & & & 2\mu+U & t & & & & \\ \bra{,1,,1,} & & & t & & t & 2\mu & t & t & & \\ \bra{,1,,,1} & -t & & & t & & t & 2\mu & & t & \\ \bra{,,1,1,} & & & & & & t & & 2\mu+U & t & \\ \bra{,,1,,1} & & -t & & & & & t & t & 2\mu & t \\ \bra{,,,1,1} & & & -t & & & & & & t & 2\mu+U \\ \end{array} \nonumber \end{equation} Scroll horizontally to view the complete matrix
    All expressions above use periodic boundary conditions, so hopping and interactions between sites 1 and 5 are included by default. The matrix elements for hopping across the boundary carry $-t$. With the present ordering of the Fock basis, this sign follows from fermionic anticommutation relations; in a sector with a fixed even particle number, boundary hopping acquires an additional minus sign relative to hopping in the bulk. Diagonalizing this matrix yields the many-body energy spectrum in the chosen particle-number sector.
