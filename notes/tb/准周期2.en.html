    1. Transfer Matrices and Lyapunov Exponents

    Consider a one-dimensional nearest-neighbor tight-binding model, \begin{align} H=-t\sum_i (|i\rangle\langle i+1|+|i+1\rangle\langle i|)+\sum_i V_i |i\rangle\langle i|, \end{align} whose time-independent Schrödinger equation is \begin{align} H \ket{\psi}= E \ket{\psi} , \label{eq1} \end{align} Substituting $\ket{\psi} = \sum_i \alpha_i \ket{i}$ gives the discrete equation \begin{align} -t (\alpha_{i+1} + \alpha_{i-1}) + V_i \alpha_i = E \alpha_i. \label{eq2} \end{align} which can be rearranged as \begin{align} \begin{pmatrix} \alpha_{i+1} \\ \alpha_i \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{V_i - E}{t} & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \alpha_{i} \\ \alpha_{i-1} \end{pmatrix} . \label{eq3} \end{align} Define \begin{align} T_i = \begin{pmatrix} \frac{V_i - E}{t} & -1 \\ 1 & 0 \end{pmatrix} , \end{align} We then have \begin{align} \begin{pmatrix} \alpha_{i+1} \\ \alpha_i \end{pmatrix} = T_i ~ T_{i-1} ~ \cdots ~ T_2 \begin{pmatrix} \alpha_{2} \\ \alpha_{1} \end{pmatrix} . \label{eq5} \end{align} Given an energy $E$ and an initial vector $(\alpha_2,\alpha_1)^T$, matrix iteration yields the amplitude at any site $i$. The matrix $T_i$ is called a transfer matrix; it maps the wave-function amplitudes at sites $i-1$ and $i$ to those at sites $i$ and $i+1$. Transfer matrices can be used to study the exponential growth or decay of a wave function with distance. For a long chain, directly evaluating $T_L\cdots T_2$ rapidly overflows or underflows, so a QR decomposition is performed at every step. Taking $Q_1=I$, define the recursion \begin{align} T_i Q_{i-1}=Q_iR_i, \end{align} where $Q_i$ is orthogonal and $R_i$ is upper triangular. With repeated orthogonalization, the $a$th Lyapunov exponent is given by the average logarithm of the local stretching factor: \begin{align} \gamma_a=\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=2}^{L}\ln |(R_i)_{aa}|. \end{align} For the $2\times2$ transfer matrix considered here, $\det T_i=1$, so the two exponents sum to zero and may be written as $\gamma_1=\gamma$ and $\gamma_2=-\gamma$. If $E$ is an eigenenergy within the spectrum, the positive Lyapunov exponent gives the inverse localization length of the wave function: \begin{align} \xi^{-1}(E)=\gamma(E). \end{align} Note that the accumulated quantity is $\ln |(R_i)_{aa}|$, not $\ln |(R_i)_{aa}|^2$; the latter would introduce an extra factor of two. For the standard AAH model, $V_i=V\cos(2\pi\alpha i+\theta)$, the analytical result at energies within the spectrum is \begin{align} \gamma(E)=\max\left\{0,\ln\left|\frac{V}{2t}\right|\right\}, \end{align} so the localization transition occurs at $|V|=2|t|$.

    2. QR Algorithm

    The following Matlab program performs a stepwise QR decomposition at $E=0$ and compares the numerical Lyapunov exponent with the analytical result for the standard AAH model. 

    
clear; clc; close all;
t = 1;
E = 0;
alpha = (sqrt(5) - 1) / 2;
theta = 0;
L = 5e4;
V_sec = 0:0.05:4;
gamma_num = zeros(size(V_sec));

for iv = 1:numel(V_sec)
    V = V_sec(iv);
    Q = eye(2);
    log_growth = zeros(2,1);

    for i = 1:L
        Vi = V * cos(2*pi*alpha*i + theta);
        Ti = [(Vi-E)/t, -1; 1, 0];
        [Q,R] = qr(Ti * Q, 0);

        % Fix the arbitrary QR signs so diag(R) is non-negative
        s = sign(diag(R));
        s(s == 0) = 1;
        S = diag(s);
        Q = Q * S;
        R = S * R;

        log_growth = log_growth + log(abs(diag(R)));
    end

    exponents = sort(log_growth / L, 'descend');
    gamma_num(iv) = max(exponents(1), 0);
end

gamma_exact = max(0, log(max(V_sec, eps) / (2*abs(t))));

plot(V_sec, gamma_num, 'k-', 'LineWidth', 1.5); hold on
plot(V_sec, gamma_exact, 'r--', 'LineWidth', 1.5);
xlabel('V/t');
ylabel('Lyapunov exponent');
legend('QR numerical', 'AAH exact', 'Location', 'northwest');

    As the chain length increases, the numerical result should approach the analytical curve: for $V<2t$, $\gamma=0$, whereas for $V>2t$, $\gamma=\ln(V/2t)>0$, with the corresponding localization length $\xi=1/\gamma$.
