    1. Discretizing the Hamiltonian by Finite Differences

    We begin with the Schrödinger equation in a potential $U(x)$: \begin{align} E \psi = [- \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \nabla^2 + U(x)] \psi, \label{eq1} \end{align} For a one-dimensional chain, this becomes \begin{align} E \psi(x) = [- \frac{\hbar^2}{2m} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} + U(x)] \psi(x), \label{eq2} \end{align} We can then discretize the one-dimensional chain into lattice sites separated by a spacing a. Using \begin{align} \frac{\partial}{\partial x} \psi(x) = \frac{\psi(x+a) - \psi(x)}{a}, \label{eq3} \end{align} together with \begin{align} \frac{\partial^2}{\partial x^2} \psi(x) = [\frac{\psi(x+a) - \psi(x)}{a} & - \frac{\psi(x) - \psi(x-a)}{a}]/a = \frac{\psi(x+a) - 2\psi(x) + \psi(x-a)}{a^2}, \end{align} the Laplacian $\nabla^2$ can be expressed in finite-difference form, giving \begin{align} E \psi(x) = - \frac{\hbar^2[\psi(x+a) + \psi(x-a) -2\psi(x)]}{2ma^2} + U(x) \psi(x). \label{eq4} \end{align} Labeling the discretized lattice sites by $x = ja$, so that $\psi(x) = \psi_j$, the equation becomes \begin{align} E \psi_j = - \frac{\hbar^2[\psi_{j+1} + \psi_{j-1} -2\psi_j]}{2ma^2} + U_j \psi_j. \label{eq5} \end{align} Denoting $\dfrac{-\hbar^2}{2ma^2}$ by $t$, we obtain \begin{align} E \psi_j = t [\psi_{j+1} + \psi_{j-1} -2\psi_j] + U_j \psi_j, \label{eq6} \end{align} The Hamiltonian has now been discretized. Its matrix form is \begin{equation} E \begin{pmatrix} \psi_1 \\ \psi_2 \\ \psi_3\\ \vdots \\ \psi_N \end{pmatrix} = \begin{pmatrix} U_1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & U_2 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & U_3 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & U_N \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \psi_1 \\ \psi_2 \\ \psi_3\\ \vdots \\ \psi_N \end{pmatrix} + t \begin{pmatrix} -2 & 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 1 & -2 & 1 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & 1 & -2 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 & -2 \end{pmatrix} \begin{pmatrix} \psi_1 \\ \psi_2 \\ \psi_3\\ \vdots \\ \psi_N \end{pmatrix} \label{eq15} \end{equation} In much of the literature, the tight-binding Hamiltonian is commonly written as \begin{align} \mathcal{H} = \sum_n (U_n-2t)c_n^{\dagger}c_n + t\sum_n (c_n^{\dagger}c_{n+1}+{\rm H.c.}) \end{align} The uniform diagonal energy shift $-2t$ can be absorbed into the on-site energy or the choice of energy zero, so this constant shift is often omitted. Moreover, because $t=-\hbar^2/(2ma^2)$, the nearest-neighbor hopping is also frequently written as $-t_0$, where $t_0=\hbar^2/(2ma^2)>0$.
